This is a candid, carefully hedged negative-result paper, and its honesty about post hoc choices is a strength. Its conclusions are weaker than its framing, though. The central null carries little evidential weight because no arm, not even train-on-gold, ever improves, and the training setup (loss over the prompt, a 512-token limit that truncates about a quarter of the pool) may itself damage the model. The adaptation audit conditions on the \boxed share, which is the very channel through which adaptation would show, and it has almost no power. The detectable-effect and 2.2-point bounds are post hoc and omit seed variance. Several numbers in the text and tables disagree. These are fixable by adding a positive control and a dose-matched gold-defect control, correcting the numbers, and narrowing the title and claims. The abstract placeholders and unresolved supplementary references are noted as work in progress.
