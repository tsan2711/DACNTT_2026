The paper is unusually careful about its own null results (held-out exam, problem-level intervals, explicit post hoc labels, honest reporting of readings that did not hold up), and the core negative finding — no detectable gain from this LoRA SFT self-training recipe on a 1.5B model — is sound within its stated power limits. The problem is the headline contribution: the "direction-free" r_t test is not novel (Egashira et al. and TinyV already track false negatives on the policy's own rollouts over training), its instantiation on the gold-storage defect cannot measure writing adaptation (the defect is fixed by the problem, unboxed outputs enter only the denominator, unparseable golds are mixed in), it has no interval or positive control, and the saved data actually show r_t declining in all three runs, contradicting "no downward trend". Secondary issues are the absence of a positive reference for the recipe, a "does not improve" framing that exceeds a ~9-point minimum detectable effect, and several range/interval misstatements. These are fixable, but the title and contribution framing need to be rebuilt around what the data support.
