The paper is unusually candid: it reports a null, discloses post hoc choices and states its own power limits, and the arithmetic I recomputed (Wilson interval, t-intervals, detectable-effect constant, table identities) is largely correct. The central weakness is that the null cannot support its framing. The failed positive gate (reference-solution training collapses by 32 points) points to a pipeline defect, so no result can be attributed to self-training or to the verifier; every score is graded by the defective verifier; and the pre-set measures have little power. The r_t audit is partly an algebraic identity (boxed-only r_t × boxed share), and the injected-bias controls are neither valid positive controls nor consistently reported. Standards are also applied unevenly (no multiplicity correction, small declines read as real). These issues are fixable mostly by reframing and cheap re-scoring with V' on saved generations.
